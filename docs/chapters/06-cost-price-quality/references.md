# References: Cost, Price, and Quality

1. [Total cost of ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_cost_of_ownership) - Wikipedia - Defines TCO as an estimate of the direct and indirect costs of owning a product, and argues that ownership costs far exceed the purchase price. It supports the chapter's three-year TCO calculation.

2. [Break-even point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Break-even_point) - Wikipedia - Explains the point at which total cost equals total revenue, with sections on purpose, construction, and limitations. It underlies the chapter's break-even volume formula comparing a local machine's fixed cost with a per-book price.

3. [Depreciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depreciation) - Wikipedia - Covers depreciation as both a fall in an asset's value and the allocation of its cost over its useful life, with the common calculation methods. It supports the chapter's even three-year spread of hardware cost.

4. Quality Is Free: The Art of Making Quality Certain (1st Edition, 1979) - Philip B. Crosby - McGraw-Hill - Crosby defined quality as conformance to requirements and measured it by the price of nonconformance: rework, scrap, and lost customers. That idea shows why a cheaper local model whose output must be redone has not saved money.

5. Horngren's Cost Accounting: A Managerial Emphasis (16th Edition) - Srikant M. Datar and Madhav V. Rajan (original author Charles T. Horngren) - Pearson - A long-running cost accounting text built on the theme "different costs for different purposes," a phrase credited to economist J. M. Clark. Its cost-volume-profit chapters match the chapter's fixed, variable, and break-even analysis.

6. [3.2 Calculate a Break-Even Point in Units and Dollars](https://openstax.org/books/principles-managerial-accounting/pages/3-2-calculate-a-break-even-point-in-units-and-dollars) - OpenStax - Free textbook section from Principles of Accounting, Volume 2 that derives break-even in units and dollars from fixed costs and contribution margin. It matches the chapter's break-even formula and worked examples.

7. [Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.B: Average Price of Electricity to Ultimate Customers by End-Use Sector](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/monthly/epm_table_grapher.php?t=table_5_06_b) - U.S. Energy Information Administration - Publishes year-to-date electricity prices in cents per kilowatthour by sector and state, including a commercial column and a U.S. total. It is the kind of source behind the chapter's per-kilowatthour electricity price.

8. [Pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing) - Anthropic - Lists model prices per million tokens, with separate rates for input, output, and cached tokens. It is a concrete example of the per-token pricing that the chapter contrasts with subscription and per-seat charges.

9. [Markup](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/accounting/markup/) - Corporate Finance Institute - Defines markup as profit over cost and contrasts it with gross margin, using a worked example in which a 20 percent markup yields a gross margin under 17 percent. It matches the chapter's margin-versus-markup table.

10. [Cost Estimating and Assessment Guide (GAO-20-195G)](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-20-195g) - U.S. Government Accountability Office - Best-practice guide to building and defending cost estimates, with steps for ground rules and assumptions, data collection, and sensitivity and risk analysis. It models the chapter's habit of stating assumptions and varying them.
