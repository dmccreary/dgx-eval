## About This MicroSim

Every item in an experiment plays one role. The independent variable is the one thing changed on purpose, controlled variables are held fixed on purpose, measured results are recorded, and uncontrolled variables vary without anyone deciding.

This MicroSim describes one experiment and shows ten items from it, including two that look controlled and are not. The items are synthetic examples drawn from the design of this evaluation.

## How to Use

- Read the experiment, then the item. Ask who set it, and whether it was held the same on purpose.
- Choose one of the four roles. Press **Confirm** to lock in the answer. The correct answer and the reason appear, and there is no second attempt.
- After the last one, read the score and the misconception named beside each miss, then press **Start over** to try again.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will differentiate the role of each of 10 items in a model-comparison experiment as independent variable, controlled variable, measured result, or uncontrolled variable, with at least 9 of 10 correct.

### Grade Level

Professional development and adult continuing education

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

- Independent variable
- Controlled variables
- Uncontrolled variable
- Metric selection
- Fixed inputs

### Activities

1. **Work alone** (5 min): Answer every item without notes, committing each answer before the reveal.
2. **Review the misses** (3 min): For each miss, read the reason and the misconception on the final screen, then find the passage in the chapter that corrects it.
3. **Discuss** (2 min): Items 9 and 10 both mention a model's default setting, and have different roles. State the difference.

### Assessment

Mastery is 9 of 10 correct on the first attempt. Exploring before answering is practice and does not count as evidence.

### Misconceptions Addressed

- Anything that is the same in every run is controlled, whether or not it was decided.
- A measured result can also be the independent variable.
- A variable that cannot be controlled can be ignored.
- The model's default setting counts as controlled.
