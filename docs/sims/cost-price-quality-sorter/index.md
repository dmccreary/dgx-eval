## About This MicroSim

Cost is what an owner pays to own and operate a machine. Price is what a provider charges a customer. Quality is how good the output is. The three are easy to mix up, especially when a statement contains a dollar figure.

This MicroSim shows ten statements, some in dollars and some not, so that the unit of a number is no shortcut. The statements are synthetic examples.

## How to Use

- Read the statement and choose **Cost**, **Price**, or **Quality**.
- Press **Confirm** to lock in the answer. The correct answer and the reason appear, and there is no second attempt.
- After the last one, read the score and the misconception named beside each miss, then press **Start over** to try again.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will classify each of 10 statements about generating a textbook as describing a cost, a price, or a quality, with at least 9 of 10 correct.

### Grade Level

Professional development and adult continuing education

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

- Cost
- Price
- Quality

### Activities

1. **Work alone** (5 min): Answer every item without notes, committing each answer before the reveal.
2. **Review the misses** (3 min): For each miss, read the reason and the misconception on the final screen, then find the passage in the chapter that corrects it.
3. **Discuss** (2 min): Statement 8 mentions both a cost and a margin. Explain why the statement as a whole describes a price.

### Assessment

Mastery is 9 of 10 correct on the first attempt. Exploring before answering is practice and does not count as evidence.

### Misconceptions Addressed

- A number in dollars must be a price.
- A service's charge tells you what delivering the service costs it.
- A generated text that builds without errors is good.
