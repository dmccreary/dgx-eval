    Ratios¶

    Ratios compare two quantities by showing how many times one value contains another. They are expressed as a colon (e.g., 3:2) or as a fraction.

    Worked Example¶

    A recipe calls for 3 cups of flour for every 2 cups of sugar. If you have 9 cups of flour, how many cups of sugar are needed?

    Solution: The ratio flour:sugar is 3:2. For 9 cups of flour, the factor is 9/3 = 3. Multiply the sugar part of the ratio by this factor: 2 × 3 = 6 cups of sugar.

    Proportions¶

    A proportion states that two ratios are equal. It is used to solve problems where the relationship between quantities is constant.

    Worked Example¶

    If 5 pencils cost $2, how much do 12 pencils cost?

    Solution: Set up the proportion 5/2 = 12/x. Cross‑multiply: 5x = 24 → x = 24/5 = $4.80.

    Percentages¶

    Percentages express a number as a fraction of 100. They are used to compare parts of a whole.

    Worked Example¶

    What is 25% of 80?

    Solution: Convert 25% to decimal 0.25. Multiply: 0.25 × 80 = 20.

    Variable Substitution¶

    Variable substitution replaces a variable with a specific value to simplify expressions.

    Worked Example¶

    Simplify 2x + 3 when x = 4.

    Solution: Substitute x = 4 → 2·4 + 3 = 8 + 3 = 11.

    Direct Variation¶

    Direct variation describes a relationship where y = kx, with k constant.

    Worked Example¶

    If y varies directly with x and y = 12 when x = 3, find y when x = 5.

    Solution: k = y/x = 12/3 = 4. Then y = 4·5 = 20.

    Inverse Variation¶

    Inverse variation describes a relationship where y = k/x.

    Worked Example¶

    If y varies inversely with x and y = 6 when x = 2, find y when x = 3.

    Solution: k = y·x = 6·2 = 12. Then y = 12/3 = 4.

    Coordinate Plane¶

    The coordinate plane consists of two perpendicular axes, the x‑axis and y‑axis, intersecting at the origin.

    Worked Example¶

    Plot the point (3, -2) on the coordinate plane.

    Solution: Move 3 units right on the x‑axis, then 2 units down on the y‑axis.
