    Chapter Quality Score¶

    Last updated October 11, 2026.

    This page describes the program that gives each generated chapter a score from 0 to 100, and gives each book a score for how consistent its chapters are. The program is one Python file, chapter_quality.py, and it is meant to fill the Automated Quality Score column of Evaluation Status, which is empty so far.

    A conformance score, not a quality verdict

    The score measures how closely a chapter follows the book's written content rules, using counts a program can reproduce. It does not check that the mathematics is correct or that the explanation is any good. No person has yet compared its output with their own judgment of the chapters, so read it as a screen that points to chapters worth reading, not as a ranking of how well the models teach.

    What the Score Has to Do¶

    The Primary Author set four requirements:

    • Use objective measurements.
    • Include adherence to the rules in CONTENT-GENERATION-GUIDE.md, and include the precise placement of diagrams and the quality of their specifications.
    • Report word count, diagram count, and reading level for each chapter.
    • Favor consistent quality over high absolute quality.

    Three more constraints follow from how the benchmark runs. The same files must always give the same score, so a change in the score means a change in the chapter. The program must run on a DGX with nothing installed beyond the Python standard library. And every number must be explainable: a chapter that scores badly has to show which rule it broke.

    The Algorithm in One Page¶

    1. Read the chapter. Split it into blocks: headings, paragraphs, lists, tables, code, admonitions, and <details> diagram specifications.
    2. Decide whether it is written. A chapter that still holds the placeholder text, or has fewer than 1,000 words, is a stub and scores 0.
    3. Compute its word budget from the Concept Impact Scores in its own Concepts Covered table.
    4. Score six dimensions, each from 0 to 1, each built from a handful of sub-scores.
    5. Combine them with fixed weights into the chapter score.
    6. Score the book. Multiply the book's mean chapter score by how little its written chapters differ from one another.
    Dimension Weight The question it asks
    Budget 20 Does the chapter hold the words, worked examples, and non-text elements that its own concepts call for?
    Diagram placement 20 Is each diagram introduced, defined before it appears, near the text it illustrates, and spread through the chapter?
    Diagram specification 20 Does each specification carry the fields, and the precision, that the specification rules ask for?
    Format 15 Does the Markdown render, and are the headings, admonitions, and mascot used as the guide says?
    Padding 10 Does the chapter repeat itself or pad with filler?
    Reader fit 15 Is the reading level right for the audience, with equations and worked examples present?

    Step 1: Reading a Chapter¶

    The program does not render Markdown. It walks the file line by line and cuts it into top-level blocks: headings, paragraphs, lists, tables, fenced code, display math, HTML comments, raw HTML, admonitions, and <details> blocks. Frontmatter is split off first and read for title and description.

    Finding the diagram specifications¶

    A <details> block is a diagram specification when any one of these holds:

    • A #### Diagram: heading owns it.
    • It has at least three of the eighteen specification field labels.
    • Its summary starts with the word Diagram.
    • It is a reused MicroSim: its summary says so, or it contains an <iframe>.

    Other <details> blocks, such as a hidden answer, are ignored. The third test exists because one model wrote <summary>#### Diagram: Title</summary> with a prose paragraph and no fields inside. That is a diagram in name only, and it should score as a bad specification, not disappear.

    A heading owns a block by the same rule the skill's own batch tool uses. create-microsim-todo-json-files.py splits a chapter on #### Diagram: headers and takes the first <details> block after each. Prose, an iframe, or a fullscreen button may sit between the two. The score follows that rule and adds one limit: the search for a header stops at another <details> block or at a heading of level 1, 2, or 3, because a diagram under a new section is not placed by the previous section's header.

    Counting words¶

    Two word counts are kept, both split on whitespace as the guide counts them.

    • Total words is the whole file after the frontmatter. It matches the Words column of the chapter metrics pages.
    • Body words is what a student reads: everything except the specification blocks, fenced code, and the Concepts Covered table. The budget uses this one.

    The stub gate¶

    A chapter is a stub when the placeholder text TODO: Generate Chapter Content is present or the file has fewer than 1,000 words. The rule is imported from score_chapters.py, so there is one definition of "written" in the project. A stub scores 0 and none of the other checks run. Its raw counts are still recorded.

    Step 2: The Word Budget¶

    The guide sizes a chapter from its concepts, not from a flat number. Each concept has a Concept Impact Score (CIS): one plus the sum of the CIS of every concept that depends on it. A concept many others build on needs more careful explanation, so its importance is normalized against the book's largest CIS:

    \[ E(c) = \frac{\ln(\mathrm{CIS}(c) + 1)}{\ln(\mathrm{CIS}_{\max} + 1)} \]

    The program reads CIS_max from the book's learning-graph.json. If that file is missing it falls back to the largest CIS in the chapters' own tables and says so in its output. E(c) then sets a tier:

    Tier E(c) Target words Required elements
    A 0.5 or more 500–750 worked example, plus a diagram, chart, table, or MicroSim
    B 0.2 to 0.5 250–400 worked example
    C under 0.2 120–200 a clear definition

    A chapter's budget is the sum of its concepts' targets, not a number of its own. A chapter of mostly Tier A concepts is expected to run several times longer than one of mostly Tier C. That variation is intended and the score does not normalize it away.

    A plateau, not a target¶

    The guide calls the budget "guidelines, not requirements" and says a dense chapter beats a padded one at the target length. So the word-fit score does not peak at the target. Full credit is a wide plateau:

    score
1.0 |         ______________________
    |        /                      \
    |       /                        \
0.0 |______/                          \_________
         0.2L   0.6L          1.25H   2.5H      body words
         L = sum of the low targets, H = sum of the high targets

    A chapter below 20% of the low target scores 0. It reaches full credit at 60% of the low target, keeps it up to 125% of the high target, and falls to 0 at 250% of the high target. A chapter is never rewarded for exceeding its budget.

    If a chapter has no Concepts Covered table, its budget cannot be computed. The whole dimension scores 0 and the chapter is flagged NO_CONCEPT_TABLE.

    Step 3: The Six Dimensions¶

    Each dimension is a weighted mean of sub-scores between 0 and 1. A sub-score that does not apply to a chapter is left out and the remaining weights are rescaled, so a chapter with no admonitions is not penalized for their absence.

    Budget (weight 20)¶

    Sub-score Weight How it is measured
    Words fit 0.35 Body words against the plateau above
    Worked examples 0.20 Lines that start with Worked Example or Example, against 60% of the Tier A plus Tier B concept count
    Non-text elements 0.15 Diagram specs, embedded sims, and tables, against 60% of the Tier A concept count. Skipped when there are no Tier A concepts
    Concept coverage 0.20 The share of listed concepts whose label appears in the prose, after lower-casing and light stemming
    Title alignment 0.10 1 if any non-generic word of the title appears in the concept labels or headings

    The last two checks catch a failure that word counts cannot. One model's chapter titled Exponents and Radicals held a concept list about function notation. Words such as foundations, introduction, and advanced are ignored in titles, because they match nothing.

    Diagram placement (weight 20)¶

    Four of these are scored per diagram and averaged over the chapter. The other three describe the chapter as a whole. A chapter with no diagram specification scores 0 on all seven and is flagged NO_DIAGRAMS.

    Sub-score Weight How it is measured
    Header 0.25 1 for an exact #### Diagram: Title heading, 0.5 for a Diagram heading at another level or format, 0 for none
    Bridge 0.15 A paragraph of 8 or more words right before the header, or between header and block. 1 if its last two sentences signpost the diagram, 0.5 if not
    Terms defined 0.20 Each term in the block's Prerequisites field is found in the teaching text above the block. 1 for the whole phrase, 0.5 if every word appears, 0 otherwise
    Relevance 0.15 The content words of the title and Learning Objective appear in the 300 words before the block. Full credit at 50%
    Spacing 0.10 Paragraphs beyond four in a row with no non-text element between them, as a share of all paragraphs. Full credit at none, zero at half
    Evenness 0.10 The ideal gap, one over the number of diagrams plus one, divided by the largest gap between diagrams, measured in body words
    Density 0.05 Diagrams per 1,000 body words: full credit from 0.8 to 4.0, zero at 0 and at 8

    Terms defined and Relevance are the two checks that test whether a diagram sits in the right place. Both read the teaching text only. The Summary, Concepts Covered, and Prerequisites sections are excluded, because the concept table would otherwise "define" every term in the chapter.

    Diagram specification (weight 20)¶

    Each specification block is scored on 22 checks drawn from the rules R1 to R14 in the skill's microsim-specification-rules.md. The chapter's score is the mean over its blocks. A reused MicroSim, which has no fields of its own, receives full marks.

    Checks Weight What they test Rule
    Fields present, content specific 3 each All 18 fields exist. The Content field has no "for example", "such as", or "etc." and holds a table, a list, or at least 40 words R4
    Bloom, evidence rule, rules model, feedback 2 each Level is one of the six names and the verb is in that level's list. Evidence names a criterion. Rules hold a formula, comparator, or number. Feedback covers correct, incorrect, and attempts R1, R2, R6, R8
    Enums, activity steps, interactive, no build details 1.5 each Type and Library come from the permitted lists. Activity is a numbered list. It contains an interaction verb. No pixel sizes, colors, canvas, or library calls R7, R12, R14
    Label format, type and library pair, objective form, provenance, starting state, anchors, no placeholder, sim-id, depth 1 each Bold labels and <br/> endings. Library suits Type. Objective begins "The learner will" with no banned verb. Provenance states a source. Anchors' numbers appear in the chapter. No TODO or unfilled template. sim-id is kebab-case and unique. Spec length is 150 to 1,200 words R1, R5, R10, R14
    Impossible features, summary matches, status 0.5 each No mouse-wheel zoom or clickable Mermaid edges. The summary matches the heading. Status is Specified or Reused R13

    The Type and Library checks compare exactly against the lists in rule R14, so mermaid fails where Mermaid passes. R14 says a value outside the list "silently falls back to p5.js". Whether the downstream generator also ignores case has not been tested. If it does, this check is stricter than it needs to be.

    Format (weight 15)¶

    Sub-score Weight How it is measured
    Lists render 0.25 One minus the share of lists with no blank line before them, which the site shows as a run-on paragraph
    No leaked scaffolding 0.15 Text the model wrote for itself, such as (Tier C: 120-200 words) or an Elaboration Budget table. Full credit at none, zero at three
    Headings 0.15 One H1, no skipped levels, and the Summary, Concepts Covered, and Prerequisites sections present
    Admonition bodies 0.10 Indented four spaces
    Images and embeds 0.10 Markdown image targets exist. An iframe pointing at a placeholder host, such as example.com, fails
    Details blocks 0.10 Each carries markdown="1", without which its contents are not rendered
    Well formed 0.10 Fences and <details> tags are balanced and the file does not end mid-sentence
    Frontmatter 0.05 title and description are present
    Mascot 0.20 The placement rules in the guide, checked only where the book defines a mascot

    The mascot check covers the guide's pose counts, no two admonitions back-to-back, the image in every admonition body, 1 to 3 sentences of text, opening with a welcome, and closing with a celebration. A book defines a mascot when docs/img/mascot/ exists. In a book that defines none, a chapter that uses a mascot- admonition anyway scores 0 on this sub-score and is flagged.

    Math delimiters are not scored. $…$, \(…\), $$…$$, and \[…\] are all accepted, because mkdocs.yml renders each of them.

    Padding (weight 10)¶

    Sub-score Weight How it is measured
    Duplicate sentences 0.25 Sentences of six or more words that repeat another. Full credit at 2% or less, zero at 15%
    Near-duplicate paragraphs 0.15 Paragraphs sharing 70% of their four-word phrases with another. Full credit at none, zero at 25%
    Compression 0.20 zlib ratio averaged over 600-word windows. Full credit at 0.30 or more, zero at 0.18
    Filler phrases 0.20 A list of stock phrases such as "it is important to note" and "delve", per 1,000 words. Full credit at 1, zero at 6
    Templated specs 0.20 The share of specs that are not copies of a sibling: two or more identical fields, or the same title

    The compression ratio is taken over fixed windows because a whole-chapter ratio falls as a chapter grows. An 8,000-word chapter compresses to about 0.27 and a 1,000-word one to about 0.38, so a whole-chapter ratio would punish length itself.

    Reader fit (weight 15)¶

    Sub-score Weight How it is measured
    Reading grade 0.40 Flesch-Kincaid grade of the prose with math, code, tables, and specs removed. Full credit from two grades below the target to one and a half above, zero five away. The target is 9.0 for Algebra I
    Sentence length 0.15 Full credit from 10 to 20 words per sentence, zero at 6 and at 32
    Math present 0.20 Equations per 1,000 words: full credit from 3 to 80, zero at 0 and at 250
    Worked-example density 0.15 Worked examples per 1,000 words, full credit at 1
    Variety 0.10 Distinct kinds of element used: list, table, diagram, admonition, code, display math, image. Full credit at four

    The reading grade uses the textstat package when it is installed and a built-in syllable estimate when it is not, and the output says which. On the 49 written chapters the two differ by 0.47 grade levels on average, which moves the chapter score by 0.22 points on average and by 2.5 points at most.

    Step 4: From Sub-Scores to One Number¶

    Every sub-score is one of three shapes: a plateau with sloping sides, a threshold that rises to full credit, or a threshold that falls from it. All are linear and clamped between 0 and 1, so they can be read by eye and changed by editing one constant.

    The chapter score is the weighted mean of its six dimension scores:

    \[ Q = 100 \times \frac{\sum_d w_d \, s_d}{\sum_d w_d} \]

    with the weights in the first table of this page.

    Step 5: The Book Score¶

    A book gets a level, a consistency, and a headline that combines the two.

    \[ \mathrm{level} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} Q_i \qquad \mathrm{consistency} = 100 \left(1 - \frac{\sigma_W}{\mu_W}\right) \qquad \mathrm{headline} = \frac{\mathrm{level} \times \mathrm{consistency}}{100} \]

    Here N is every chapter in the book, with stubs counted as 0, and μ_W and σ_W are the mean and population standard deviation of the written chapters only. Consistency needs at least two written chapters; with one it is undefined and so is the headline.

    The program also reports the floor, the lowest written chapter, and a robust z-score for each written chapter: its distance from the median divided by 1.4826 times the median absolute deviation, with the divisor never below 2 points. With at least four written chapters, a z-score of 2 or more in either direction is flagged OUTLIER_LOW or OUTLIER_HIGH. Finally it reports each dimension's mean and coefficient of variation, which show which kind of failure a book is inconsistent about.

    A Worked Example¶

    Chapter 3, Linear Equations, from gemma4:31b. It has 16 concepts: none in Tier A, 5 in Tier B, 11 in Tier C. So its budget is 5 × 250 + 11 × 120 = 2,570 words at the low end and 5 × 400 + 11 × 200 = 4,200 at the high end.

    Dimension How the number arises Score
    Budget 1,296 body words. Full credit starts at 0.6 × 2,570 = 1,542 and zero is at 514, so words fit is (1,296 − 514) ÷ (1,542 − 514) = 0.76. Worked examples, concept coverage, and title all score 1. Non-text is skipped because there is no Tier A concept 90.1
    Placement Header 1.0, bridge 0.3, terms 0.95, relevance 1.0, spacing 1.0, evenness 0.56, density 1.0. Weighted: 0.84 84.1
    Specification Five blocks, mean 0.909. Each loses a little on a few checks, such as no stated rule in the evidence of mastery 90.9
    Format 21 of 35 lists lack a blank line, so lists score 0.4. Admonitions and images do not apply and are skipped 81.2
    Padding No repetition, compression 0.41, no filler 100.0
    Reader fit Grade 9.5, 12.4 words per sentence, 69 equations per 1,000 words. Variety 0.67 96.7
    \[ Q = \frac{20(90.1) + 20(84.1) + 20(90.9) + 15(81.2) + 10(100) + 15(96.7)}{100} = 89.7 \]

    For the book, gemma4:31b has 12 chapters. Ten are written, with scores from 68.7 to 90.8 and a mean of 83.8, and two are stubs. Level is the sum of those ten scores over all twelve, 69.8. The coefficient of variation of the written chapters is 0.09, so consistency is 91.1 and the headline is 69.8 × 0.911 = 63.6.

    Why It Is Built This Way¶

    Counts, not a language model¶

    A model asked to rate a chapter would be quicker to build and would notice things a regular expression cannot, such as an explanation that is wrong. It would also give a different answer on each run, bring its own biases toward text that sounds like itself, and, if it ran in the cloud, send the generated books to a third party. The rules to be checked are written, specific, and mostly countable. A program can check those, repeat itself exactly, and run on the DGX.

    The cost is real. A counting score can be satisfied by text that has the right fields and nothing in them. Section "What It Cannot Tell You" returns to this.

    Measure against the chapter's own expectation¶

    A raw count rewards whoever writes the most. A chapter on a foundational topic should be long and full of diagrams, and a chapter on a narrow topic should not. So every sub-score compares the chapter to a number derived from its own concept table, and every sub-score saturates. Adding another diagram, or another thousand words, earns nothing once the chapter is on the plateau.

    The check that this worked: across the 49 written chapters, the Spearman correlation of the score with body words is −0.10, with diagram count 0.13, and with equation count 0.15. The score does not track size.

    The cost is that the plateau's edges are judgments. The 60% and 125% limits come from the guide's own wording, not from data, and a different reading of "guidelines, not requirements" would move them.

    Multiply level by consistency¶

    The Primary Author asked to favor consistency over absolute quality. Several ways of doing that were weighed.

    Option What it does Why it was not chosen
    Mean chapter score Ignores spread A book of 10 excellent chapters and 10 stubs ties a book of 20 middling ones
    Mean plus a variance penalty Subtracts a term for spread The penalty trades points for spread at an invented exchange rate, in the same units as the score
    Lowest chapter only Very strict One bad chapter in twenty decides the whole book
    Geometric mean of level and consistency Rewards both Too generous to a uniformly poor book: level 20 with consistency 95 scores 44, where level × consistency gives 19
    Level × consistency Chosen A uniformly poor book stays poor, and a lopsided book is cut by its spread

    Stubs enter the level as 0 but are left out of the consistency. A stub is already punished by the level, and counting it again as variance would charge a book twice for one fact.

    The cost is that consistency rewards a book that wrote fewer chapters uniformly. gpt-oss:120b has the highest consistency of any book, 95.3, because it wrote only 6 of 10 chapters and those six resemble one another. The level holds it back. That is why the results table below shows both numbers, and why the written count is always printed beside them.

    Follow the skill's own parser¶

    The first version required a diagram's header to sit directly above its <details> block. The skill's own batch tool accepts prose between them, and gemma4:31b used that layout throughout chapter 3. Together with the second bug described below, the stricter rule gave the chapter 15 out of 100 on placement against over 80 for its neighbors, and cost it 14 points overall: 75 against 89.

    A score should agree with the toolchain that consumes the output, not with a stricter idea of what the toolchain ought to accept. A diagram that the batch tool will find and build is correctly placed. The score now mirrors the tool and documents the one place where it is stricter.

    Other decisions¶

    Decision Alternatives Reason Cost
    Weights set by hand: 20, 20, 20, 15, 10, 15 Fit to human ratings No human ratings exist yet The weights are opinions. A ±40% change in every weight flips the order of two pairs, named under How It Was Checked
    Padding weight lowered from 15 to 10 Keep 15 Every book averages above 90 on it, so it adds points without separating anyone It would catch a model that did start to pad, but it is now worth less
    Thresholds read off the six existing books Derive from theory Compression, density, and sentence length have no authoritative limit The limits fit this corpus. Revisit them when new models or a second course are added
    Plain regular expressions and stemming An NLP library Every rule can be read and audited in a few lines, and the file has no dependencies It misses paraphrase. "Linear function" and "a function that is linear" do not match
    One file, standard library only Several modules, packages It copies to a fresh DGX and runs It is long, about 1,300 lines, and regex-heavy
    A sub-score that does not apply is dropped Score it 0 or 1 A chapter with no images should not gain or lose points for them A chapter can avoid a check by not having the thing
    Mascot rules only where a mascot is defined Always score them The Algebra I books have no mascot, and no chapter could pass The rule is silent in a book that forgot its mascot
    Math delimiters unscored Penalize $…$ The Primary Author chose $…$ for the algebra books and the site renders it A model that writes \(…\) is not distinguished from one that writes $…$

    What It Cannot Tell You¶

    • Correctness. A chapter can follow every rule and teach a wrong procedure. Nothing here evaluates the mathematics.
    • Gaming. A model could fill every specification field with plausible keywords and score well. The checks for templated specs, repeated sentences, and thin fields narrow the gap, and nothing closes it. In one gpt-oss chapter the Misconceptions field of all twenty specifications reads "(common misconceptions)". The duplicate check caught that. No check reads the words.
    • Absence of a human baseline. The score is built on the written rules, not on what a teacher would call good. Until someone rates a sample of chapters and the two are compared, the weights are untested opinions.
    • Drift in the rules. The specification checks encode version 1.11 of the skill. A new version of the skill can make a check wrong without any change in the chapters.
    • A thin sample. Six models, 49 written chapters, and one course. The thresholds were not tested on anything else.

    How It Was Checked¶

    Check Result
    The same input scored twice Identical output
    textstat against the built-in reading estimate 0.22 points of chapter score on average, 2.5 at most
    Correlation of score with size −0.10 with words, 0.13 with diagrams, 0.15 with equations
    Every weight multiplied by a random 0.6 to 1.4, 500 times Same order in 59% of trials. 3.3% of model pairs flip

    The flips come from two pairs: qwen3.6:35b against gemma4:31b, in 41% of the trials, and gemma4:26b against gpt-oss:120b, in 8%. The first pair is within a point of each other on the headline and should be treated as a tie.

    Three bugs got past all four checks.

    • The header rule, described above, was stricter than the toolchain.
    • The teaching-text boundary. In a chapter that puts its prose straight after the Prerequisites list, with no new ## heading, the function that finds where the teaching text begins decided the whole chapter was Prerequisites. Three placement sub-scores then came out as exactly 0 for that chapter.
    • The unfilled-slot pattern. The check for template slots such as <Title Case Name> ran case-insensitively and so matched the HTML tag <summary>. It failed 278 of 282 specification blocks, and took about three points off every specification score.

    None of these showed in the totals. The first two were found by reading one chapter whose sub-scores looked wrong. The third was found because a check that fails 99% of the time is a defect, which became visible only when the failure rate of every check was tabulated. That is the argument for the comparison with human judgment that is still owed.

    First Results¶

    These are the six books of the Algebra I benchmark as scored on October 11, 2026, by the version of the program in the repository that day.

    Model Written Level Consistency Floor Headline
    qwen3.6:35b 11 of 11 76.4 84.1 55.9 64.3
    gemma4:31b 10 of 12 69.8 91.1 68.7 63.6
    nemotron-3.5-lightning:30b 12 of 12 66.1 79.3 35.2 52.5
    gemma4:26b 10 of 14 53.4 79.1 54.5 42.3
    gpt-oss:120b 6 of 10 40.9 95.3 63.5 39.0
    qwen3.8:27b 0 of 14 0 — 0 —

    Beyond the totals, the program found defects that were each confirmed by reading the chapter:

    • Chapter titles that do not match their concept lists, in nemotron.
    • The model's own working, such as (Tier C: 120-200 words), printed in headings.
    • Concepts Covered tables that were dropped from several chapters.
    • An <iframe> pointing at microsims.example.com, an invented host.
    • Twenty specifications in a single gpt-oss chapter that share identical text in six fields.

    Running It and Changing It¶

    python3 src/scoring/chapter_quality.py docs/models/<model> --raw

    Give it several books and it ends with a comparison table. --json writes every raw count and sub-score, --csv writes one row per chapter, and --target-grade sets the reading grade for a course other than Algebra I. The weights, tier table, and every threshold are constants at the top of the file.

    Each chapter's flags say which rule it broke:

    Flag Meaning
    STUB Placeholder text, or under 1,000 words. The chapter scores 0
    UNDER_BUDGET, OVER_BUDGET Body words below half the low target, or above twice the high target
    NO_CONCEPT_TABLE No Concepts Covered table, so no budget can be computed
    TITLE_MISMATCH No word of the title appears in the concepts or headings
    NO_DIAGRAMS No diagram specification in the chapter
    NO_HEADER:n n specifications with no #### Diagram: header
    BARE_DIAGRAM_HEADER:n n Diagram headers with no specification below them
    THIN_SPEC The mean specification score is under 0.5
    TEMPLATED:n n specifications that copy a sibling
    SCAFFOLD_LEAK:n n pieces of the model's working left in the text
    LISTS:n n lists with no blank line before them
    IMG_BROKEN:n n image targets that do not exist
    IFRAME_FAKE_HOST:n n embeds pointing at a placeholder host
    TRUNCATED The file ends mid-sentence
    MASCOT_UNDEFINED A mascot admonition in a book that defines no mascot
    OUTLIER_LOW, OUTLIER_HIGH The score is two or more robust deviations from the book's median
